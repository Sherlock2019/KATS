What Kepner-Tregoe Problem Analysis is

KT is a structured method for finding the true cause of a deviation instead of guessing at it. Its central idea: a problem is a deviation from expected performance, and the cause must explain both what IS happening and what IS NOT happening.

That second half is what most teams skip. If your suspected cause would also break something that is currently working fine, it is not the cause. This is why KT beats "5 Whys" on real incidents: 5 Whys assumes one linear chain and never tests alternatives, so it comfortably walks you to a plausible but wrong answer. KT forces you to compare the failing case against a working twin.

Further reading: Beyond 5 Whys: Problem Solving Skills for Real Life

Why we ask what we ask

We use a method called Kepner-Tregoe to find the true cause of a fault instead of guessing at it. Its central idea: a problem is a deviation from what should happen, and the real cause has to explain both what is broken and what is still working.

That second half is the part most people skip — and it is the part that saves the most time. If a suspected cause would also break something that is currently working fine, it is not the cause. So the questions about what still works are not padding: they are usually what points us straight at the answer.

You do not need to diagnose anything. Describe what you see, and be specific about the comparison — that is all we need to start.

The 4 things that matter most
1 Describe the deviation
Step 2 — What is wrong. One sentence for what should happen and one for what actually happens. No theories yet. Paste the exact error message: it is the key that finds a fix we may already have.
2 Bound it: what IS vs what is NOT
Step 4 — What still works. Tell us what is affected and what is comparable but healthy — another node, site, image, tenant or user. The gap between the two is where the cause hides.
3 Find the distinction
Step 4 — What is different. Put the broken case beside the working one and list how they differ. The shortest list of differences is our candidate cause list.
4 Say what changed, and what you tried
Step 5 — Evidence & history. Most faults follow a change: a patch, a deploy, a new image. Tell us what changed and what you already tried, one thing at a time, so we do not repeat it.

            🔴 IMPACT → 🚦 PRIORITIZE → 🛡️ CONTAIN → 🎯 DEFINE → 🔎 NARROW → 🧪 TEST → ✅ CONFIRM → 🔧 FIX & LEARN
          

          

            Stop the impact. Narrow the difference. Test one variable.
            Prove the cause. Fix it forever.
          


          

            

              
                

                  Stage
                  One question
                  One output
                  Where the work happens

                    Priority + blast radius + evidence captured
                    §10.2

                
🚦 PRIORITIZEWhat must we protect or restore first?
                    The action to do first, and why it beat the others
                    §10.2

                
🛡️ CONTAINHow do we reduce impact safely now?

                    §10.5

            It is not a line. NARROW → TEST → CONFIRM is a loop — every
            refuted candidate sends you back to NARROW. That is why the bar brackets those three with a
            pass counter, and why progress inside them is measured in candidates eliminated,
            not stages passed.
          

          

            Two funnels run at once: operational (max impact → min impact) through
            IMPACT/PRIORITIZE/CONTAIN, and diagnostic (max uncertainty → min uncertainty) through DEFINE→CONFIRM.
            They are deliberately separate — restoring service and explaining the fault are different jobs.

            The method — Kepner-Tregoe, the rules this tool enforces, and what it leaves behind
          
          

            

              
The method underneath

              

                Kepner-Tregoe: a problem is a deviation from expected
                performance, and the cause must explain both what IS happening
                and what IS NOT.
              

              

                That second half is what most teams skip and what does the real work. If your suspected
                cause would also break something currently working fine, it is not the cause.
              

              

                This is why KT beats 5 Whys — 5 Whys assumes one linear chain and never
                tests alternatives, so it walks you comfortably to a plausible wrong answer.
              

              
One pass of the narrowing loop

              
    
                
  1. Restate the deviation — one sentence, no theory in it.
    2. 
                
  2. Confirm WHERE / WHEN / TREND: affected against unaffected.
    3. 
                
  3. List the distinctions between "fails" and "works".
    4. 
                
  4. Pick the distinction that most cleanly separates IS from IS NOT and is safest to test.
    5. 
                
  5. Change one variable, holding everything else constant.
    6. 
                
  6. Capture the outcome and log the pass in §10.6.
    7. 
                
  7. Supports → CONFIRM. Refutes → back to NARROW. Inconclusive → improve the test.
    8. 
              

            


            

              

                Seven rules the tool enforces rather than advises
              

              
    
                
  1. Evidence is a door, not a note. It is an output of IMPACT; everything after it
                    stays blocked until something is captured. By the time anyone is mitigating, the
                    state they needed is gone.
    2. 
                
  2. Status is computed from the fields. You cannot click a stage to
                    "done". An operator can downgrade a stage; never upgrade one.
    3. 
                
  3. Mitigation is not root cause. Structurally separated — the
                    workaround lives in §10.3, the fix in §10.8. Never the same box.
    4. 
                
  4. CONFIRM is a gate, not a workspace. Three verdicts only. It passes
                    if the cause explains IS and IS NOT and toggles the symptom
                    on demand. Correlation does not pass.
    5. 
                
  5. Rank tests, not causes. Choose by chance of ending the investigation
                    per minute, and never recommend an irreversible test while a reversible one exists.
    6. 
                
  6. Shortcuts fire before any scoring. Live duplicate → do not work it.
                    Works-as-designed → do not troubleshoot. Known error → apply the fix to one target.
                    The cheapest test is the one you never run.
    7. 
                
  7. CONTAIN auto-skips when impact is genuinely bounded, so the pipeline
                    does not teach people to click past stages.
    8. 
              

            


            

              
What it leaves behind

              
    
                
  • Universal Action Record — one page, every row derived from
                    the sections, so it cannot drift from the ticket.
    • 
                
  • Loop log — one row per pass: candidate → variable changed → expected
                    → actual → verdict → next. The handover and the KB article are written from this; a
                    paragraph of notes cannot answer "what have we already tried, and what did it rule
                    out?"
    • 
                
  • KB article + Problem record — the Problem outlives the ticket and
                    owns the root cause, so recurrence becomes countable and fundable.
    • 
              

              

                The objective is never an ever-growing list of possibilities. It is to
                reduce the search space until only a defensible cause remains.
