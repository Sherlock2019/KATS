What Kepner-Tregoe Problem Analysis is

KT is a structured method for finding the true cause of a deviation instead of guessing at it. Its central idea: a problem is a deviation from expected performance, and the cause must explain both what IS happening and what IS NOT happening.

That second half is what most teams skip. If your suspected cause would also break something that is currently working fine, it is not the cause. This is why KT beats "5 Whys" on real incidents: 5 Whys assumes one linear chain and never tests alternatives, so it comfortably walks you to a plausible but wrong answer. KT forces you to compare the failing case against a working twin.

Further reading: Beyond 5 Whys: Problem Solving Skills for Real Life

Using it efficiently in this tool
1 Describe the deviation
§1.1 — one sentence for what SHOULD happen and one for what IS happening. No theories yet. Paste the exact error into 1.1.6: it is the retrieval key that finds prior cases.
2 Bound it: IS vs IS NOT
§1.2 WHERE, §1.3 WHEN, §1.4 TREND. For each, record what is affected and what is comparable but healthy. The gap between them is where the cause hides.
3 Find the distinction
§1.1.4 and §10.0.3 — put the broken case beside a working twin that differs in as few ways as possible. The shortest list of differences is your candidate cause list.
4 Test one variable
§10.3 — change ONE thing, reversibly, on ONE target. If the symptom toggles on and off with it, that is your root cause. Change two things and you have proven nothing.

10.2 KT Narrowing Checklist (one loop = one test)
  1. Restate the deviation: one sentence WHAT + expected behavior (don't mix symptoms and theories).
  2. Confirm WHERE / WHEN / TREND: affected vs unaffected (sites, tenants, hosts, AZs, images, networks, time windows).
  3. List distinctions: what differs between "fails" and "works" cases.
  4. Pick the best distinction: the one that most cleanly separates IS from IS NOT and is safest to test.
  5. Run a single-variable test: change only one thing, hold everything else constant.
  6. Capture outcome: what changed, what stayed the same, what evidence proves it.
  7. Decide next step: supports → deepen; refutes → discard; inconclusive → improve test quality.
